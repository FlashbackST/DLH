This is ransomware.

  • Malicious software that locks (encrypts) your files and asks for money to unlock them.
  • Often it steals a copy first and threatens to publish it.
  • It usually gets in through a phishing email or a stolen password.

Your files are still there, but scrambled. Only the attackers have the key.

Now what?

Option 1: Pay the ransom

Well no!

We lost money, we’re funding criminals, and there’s no guarantee we get the files back, or that they delete the stolen copy.

Option 2: Cut the connections

Right!

Cutting it off stops the ransomware from spreading to shared drives and to your backup drive. Leave the computer on and don’t try to fix it yourself.

We need to make some calls.

Good, people know. But how do we get our data back?

Let’s check our checkboxes

Who here knows the 3-2-1 rule?

3copies of your data the original + 2 backups
2kinds of storage computer + external drive
1copy kept offline unplugged, in a drawer

Google Drive or OneDrive syncing is not a backup. It syncs the locked files too.

Which copy saves us?

Sometimes.

No More Ransom, a free project by Europol and the police, offers free decryption tools for some ransomware families. Its Crypto Sheriff tool helps find a decryptor from two encrypted files and the ransom note.

Worth a try, but it only works for some ransomware. Don’t count on it. Count on your backups.
